## Abstract
```
Abstract—Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are a
foundational technology in generative AI, achieving strong perfor-
mance in content generation, but their high computational cost
limits deployment on resource-constrained devices. To expand
the feasibility of generative AI, we introduce SpikingGAN, a
framework that exploits the computational efficiency of Spiking
Neural Networks (SNNs) for efficient image generation. By lever-
aging event-driven and multiplication-free computation, Spiking-
GAN significantly reduces computational cost while maintaining
comparable image quality. ASIC implementation shows that the
proposed SpikingGAN occupies area of 3.89 × 106 μm2 with
a total power consumption of 310.48 mW. The FPGA implemen-
tation utilizes 647,745 LUTs, 135,648 FFs, 208,192 LUTRAMs,
and 1,386 BRAMs, with zero DSP usage. The design operates at
100 MHz with a total on-chip power consumption of 11.79 W.
Experimental results demonstrates that SpikingGAN achieves
19.63 − 23.91× reduction in GPU time over conventional
ANNs while maintaining comparable image quality.
Index Terms—Generative Adversarial Networks, Generative
AI, Spiking Neural Networks, Artificial Neural Networks, ASIC,
FPGA, Image Generation
```
